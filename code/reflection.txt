This assignment helped me better understand how to take messy payroll data and turn it into useful reports. One of the biggest things I learned was the importance of keeping the original data while adding cleaned columns. Using vectorized .str methods made it easier to clean employee IDs and names without going through each row individually. I also learned how pd.to_datetime with specific formats can handle different date styles, while errors="coerce" turns invalid dates into NaT. Using .dt then allowed me to create consistent month and week labels. Another important part was understanding the difference between long and wide data. I used concat with ignore_index to combine the weekly exports while keeping the correct grain of one row per employee per pay week. I also learned how group by with named aggregation could summarize employee hours and pay, and how reset_index turns the grouped results back into regular columns. Using pivot_table with fill_value=0 helped create a wide table showing hours by week, while melt converted it back into long format to identify missed weeks.
The most challenging part was connecting the different functions and making sure the outputs worked together. Running the tests helped me identify mistakes and fix them one at a time. I also learned why lineage is important because it allows us to trace records back to their original files. In the future, I would test each function immediately after writing it instead of waiting until the end. I would also check column names and data types before combining functions to avoid errors. Overall, this assignment helped me understand how data cleaning, reshaping, and reporting work together in a real business situation.
